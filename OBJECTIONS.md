# Strong objections and meaningful change conditions

The strongest objections identify a failure in an inference or a cost a proposal cannot ignore. The purpose of this register is examination, including outcomes that narrow or defeat a proposed mechanism. It is not a requirement that every critic accept MDAO's ethical commitments.

| Objection | What it challenges | What would matter next |
|---|---|---|
| A convincing memory reconstruction can increase confidence while introducing error. | The step from vividness or additional detail to accuracy, and from accuracy to original experience. | Independent comparators, error/omission denominators and procedures separating recovered information from learned or invented information. See [memory](research/MEMORY.md). |
| Finite repair may not compose into indefinitely repeatable maintenance. | The conjunction of repair coverage, integration, maintainable repair machinery and tolerable cumulative harm. | A specified maintenance cycle with net function, interactions, supporting-treatment costs and failures measured. See [biology](research/BIOLOGY.md). |
| A successor can inherit everything descriptive without being the original experiencing subject. | Promotion of resemblance, lineage or authority into first-person survival. | A defended persistence criterion and evidence discriminating its required relation from copying. Present uncertainty does not decide impossibility. See [identity](research/IDENTITY.md). |
| A durable constitution can become domination by an earlier self or an institution. | Treating old intent as permanently sufficient authority. | Concrete amendment, hardship, appeal and termination rules; a distinction between personhood and office; practical treatment of obligations to others. See [cases](CASES.md). |
| Longevity could preserve incumbency and worsen structural lock-in. | An inference from more years to more meaningful agency for everyone. | Distribution, access, entry, succession, contestability and a dignified floor. Formal permission to exit is insufficient where exit is unaffordable or harms cannot be escaped. |
| Civilizational Alignment may relabel capabilities, broad alignment or governance analysis. | Full novelty or comparative decision-superiority claims. | A matched real decision where the new framing changes a warranted choice or finds a consequential omission beyond strong alternatives. No advantage is a legitimate result. See [Q-CA-01](QUESTIONS.md#q-ca-01--does-the-wider-frame-improve-a-decision). |
| Abundance can increase consumption, concentrated power or destructive capacity without increasing altruism. | An automatic causal chain from capability to moral behavior. | A named population, intervention and counterfactual measuring usable agency and behavior separately. UA remains a proposed ethic, not the measured consequence of abundance. |
| More optionality can conflict with commitment and obligations. | Treating option count as a universal welfare metric. | Cases that distinguish voluntary commitment from coercive lock-in and include other people's claims. Viable optionality must be evaluated substantively. |
| A constitutional floor can hide disputed choices about standing and permissible intervention. | The idea that protecting future choice avoids all present moral judgment. | Explicit treatment of affected parties, uncertain moral status, legitimacy and the burden of exceptional intervention; no self-appointed universal authority. |
| Acceleration and delay can each create irreversible loss. | A universally correct speed or an unmeasured optimum. | One domain with alternatives, cumulative externalities, recovery limits and uncertainty. Maximum Sustainable Acceleration remains a directional framework until a specific model and evidence support more. |

These objections derive from the [four bounded assessments](RESEARCH.md) and the unresolved cases of the proposed framework. They have not been resolved by publication of the article. Empirical evidence can change a claim assessment; ethical argument can challenge a commitment; neither should be silently substituted for the other.

A correction should identify the affected claim, relevant evidence or premise, and the consequence for a decision. Repeated popularity, author preference and agent confidence do not establish support. Unknowns and minority interpretations remain visible when the evidence cannot settle them.
