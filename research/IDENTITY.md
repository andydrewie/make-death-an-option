# Identity, Representation and Digital Continuation

## The question

When a digital entity is built from a person's records, behavior or stated commitments, what has actually been preserved? The answer depends on the claim. A system may resemble someone, reconstruct parts of a biography, predict selected responses, carry a valid mandate, descend causally from an earlier system, or qualify as the same continuing person. None of these conclusions follows automatically from the others.

This distinction matters because present systems can already produce convincing representations and support bounded action. Those achievements are real. They do not establish that the represented person is present as the experiencing subject.

## Seven claims that require different warrants

| Claim | What it asserts | What would help warrant it | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| Representational resemblance | The entity speaks, reasons or behaves recognizably like a person on specified tasks. | Held-out tests, declared populations and comparators, error tolerances, and evidence beyond surface imitation. | Authority, endorsement, personhood or survival. |
| Autobiographical reconstruction | The entity can recover or organize facts and narratives from a life. | Accurate provenance, source reliability, causal or narrative relations appropriate to the chosen theory, and explicit uncertainty. | Ownership of the original experience or first-person memory. |
| Decision fidelity | The entity preserves specified commitments or constraints on unfamiliar choices. | Declared commitments, matched baselines, new domains, reasons or endorsement where relevant, and longitudinal drift tests. | Permission to act or general fidelity to a person. |
| Projected will | An output faithfully interprets or extends earlier governing intent. | A defined target, interpretation rule, conflict priority, revision terms, and treatment of changed circumstances. | Current lawful authority, legitimate discretion or personal identity. |
| Scoped authorization | The current entity has a valid, scoped mandate to perform specified acts. | Competent issuer, valid subject and act binding, present scope, applicable rules, and effective amendment, revocation and succession mechanisms. | Truth of every credentialed claim, moral legitimacy, personhood or survival. |
| Personal continuity | The earlier experiencing subject continues as this entity. | A defended persistence criterion and evidence that the actual transformation satisfies it. | This conclusion is not supplied by resemblance, records, prediction, provenance or authority alone. |
| Successor or lineage standing | A causally descended entity exists and may develop interests, agency or personhood of its own. | Provenance and an account of present capacities and standing. | Numerical identity with the predecessor or inherited authority over the predecessor's office and assets. |

These claims may overlap, but they are not a compulsory sequence. A successor can have standing without being the original person. A highly faithful representation can lack permission to act. An authorized agent can act without being a person or the represented individual. A purported continuation can preserve neither an old office nor every old preference.

## What current evidence supports

### Bounded representation and prediction

A study of 1,052 U.S. participants created interview-grounded generative agents and tested their answers on held-out tasks. Reported General Social Survey interview accuracy was 65.67%, compared with human retest consistency of 79.53%. Normalized performance was reported as .83 for interview questions, .82 for surveys and .86 combined, versus .74 for a demographic baseline and .71 for a brief persona baseline. The study therefore supports useful task-specific prediction from rich self-report records. It does not measure a percentage of a person reconstructed. On five economic games, the richer agents did not show a statistically significant improvement over the demographic baseline; nonsignificance is not proof of equivalence. The work did not independently validate general value preservation or first-person continuity. ([Park et al., arXiv:2411.10109v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v3))

Research on generative agents also demonstrated believable, stateful behavior from memory, reflection and planning components in a sandbox of 25 synthetic agents over two simulated days, assessed in bounded comparisons by 100 evaluators. This establishes a useful architecture demonstration. It does not establish fidelity to a real individual or continuity of a person. ([Park et al., arXiv:2304.03442v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442v2))

### Reported experiences of reconstruction and relationship

Qualitative studies show that people already use conversational systems for support, boundary negotiation and reconstruction or co-construction. One study interviewed 26 purposively recruited Chinese users with at least one month of relevant use; another studied ten mourners using chatbots in varied roles. Such interviews establish that these practices and reported experiences exist in the sampled groups. They do not establish population prevalence, clinical causation, accurate reconstruction of a deceased person, or personal continuity. ([Remember You, arXiv:2603.01017v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01017v1); [A Conversation about Loss, CHI 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581154))

### Fidelity can drift, and some drift can be reduced

Simulated persona value expression changes with model, context and conversation length. Laboratory interventions can reduce selected forms of persona or safety drift. Together, these results reject two simple assumptions: stable fidelity is not automatic, and all drift is not technically inevitable. The tested interventions still do not demonstrate preservation of a particular person's governing commitments over long periods or unfamiliar situations. ([arXiv:2402.14846v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14846v4); [arXiv:2601.10387v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387v1))

### Standards can support provenance and delegation, within their semantics

Technical standards provide parts of an assurance stack. A verifiable credential can support verification of an authentic and current issuer statement under defined rules. It does not prove the statement true or the issuer legitimate. A provenance graph can represent derivation, although a chain of generation and use does not by itself prove that the claimed history occurred. Token exchange can distinguish the present actor from a subject or earlier authority, but revocation does not propagate generically across every derived token or successor. ([W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/); [W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/); [RFC 8693](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8693))

Institutional rules can also recognize express or implied agency, apparent authority, ratification and termination effects. Data-access law can create bounded fiduciary powers, and data-protection law recognizes qualified legal bases other than consent. These examples show that explicit prospective delegation is one important route, not the only possible source of legal effects. They do not authorize a particular digital representation, establish personal endorsement, or settle jurisdiction-specific enforceability. ([UNIDROIT Principles of International Commercial Contracts](https://www.unidroit.org/instruments/commercial-contracts/unidroit-principles-2016/); [EU General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

## The central distinctions

<a id="style-resemblance-is-not-represented-authority"></a>

### Style resemblance does not establish authority to act

A system may write in a familiar voice and predict selected answers while lacking any authority to message a friend, commit time, transfer assets or enter an agreement. The stronger the action, the more important it becomes to identify the actor, the act, the mandate, the scope, the relevant rules and the current validity of the authorization.

<a id="represented-authority-is-not-current-authorization"></a>

### Historical intent does not establish current authorization

A historically accurate account of what someone once wanted may help interpret a promise or project. It does not settle what should happen after circumstances change. Current authorization requires a live institutional bridge: who may act, for what purpose, under which priority and amendment rules, and with what revocation and review. A credential proves only what its verification semantics support.

### Current authorization is not successor standing

A successor can lose the right to represent a project or control mission-bound assets while retaining possible personhood, dignity and a right to exist. Conversely, a non-person system may have a narrow valid mandate. Office and person must remain separate.

### Successor standing is not first-person continuity

A causally descended entity can learn, diverge and form relationships of its own. That may support lineage and perhaps new standing. It does not establish that the original subject is receiving the successor's experiences. If two distinct successors are equally qualified, ordinary transitive numerical identity permits at most one to be identical with the original whole. Concluding that neither is identical requires further premises; selecting one requires a selector. Lineage may be plural even when numerical identity is not.

### First-person continuity remains unknown

The evidence reviewed does not determine whether an experiencing subject can continue through reconstruction, copying, interruption or substrate change. It neither proves continuation nor proves permanent impossibility. A system that reports first-person memories may be accurate, mistaken, trained on true records, or newly conscious in its own right. The pronoun “I” does not decide which subject, if any, persisted.

## Serious philosophical routes and rivals

The disagreement is substantive because different theories require different relations:

- Psychological and causal-memory accounts ask whether the right psychological links and memory-like causal dependence continue. True records alone may be insufficient if they were merely learned by another entity. Branching creates special difficulty for nonbranching identity criteria. (Locke, cited source §§7–10, 15, 20–23, 26; Reid, modernized cited source, Essay III, chapters 4 and 6; Parfit 1971, §§II–IV, pp. 14–16.)
- Functional and organizational accounts leave open a favorable route: sufficiently fine-grained preservation of causal organization, perhaps through gradual replacement or reconstruction, could preserve what matters under the account. Ordinary persona output is much weaker than that hypothetical achievement, and instantaneous reconstruction may lack relations present in gradual replacement. (Chalmers, organizational-invariance source and cited Singularity source §§8–10.)
- Animalist and organism accounts locate persistence in the continuing living organism. A digital model does not become that organism merely by matching its outputs. (Olson, cited source §§3–4.)
- Neural-capacity or embodied-mind accounts emphasize retention of the relevant realizing basis or capacity. A duplicate may reproduce information without retaining that basis. (McMahan 1995, pp. 102–113.)
- Narrative, relational and person-life accounts require more than a fluent autobiography. They must explain how one life is individuated, including the role of communities and relationships. (Schechtman, cited introduction pp. 6–9; later defense only partially accessed.)
- First-person or nonfunctional accounts focus on the continuing perspective itself. Multiple systems saying “I” do not establish a single perspective crossing between them. (Baker, cited source pp. 10–14.)
- Reductionist approaches allow that survival-relevant psychological relations may matter even when ordinary numerical identity has no determinate or favorable answer. Practical value therefore need not collapse into identity. (Parfit 1971, §§II–IV, pp. 14–16.)

No current behavioral test adjudicates among these frameworks. The strongest favorable position is conditional: rich records, tested fidelity, causal reconstruction and institutional mechanisms could support progressively stronger forms of continuation if their extra premises are satisfied. The strongest restrictive position is also conditional: biography, predictive agreement, provenance and recognized authority can all be present while the relation required for experiential ownership or persistence remains absent.

## A responsible continuity ladder

This is the article's [six-threshold framework](../ATLAS.md#six-continuity-thresholds): durable will corresponds to Immortality of Will, subjective continuity to First-Person Continuity, and cross-substrate subjective continuity to Transcendence. The seven claims above distinguish claims and their required warrants; they are not seven continuity levels. Support for one threshold does not automatically establish another.

0. **Archive:** information persists.
1. **Durable will:** valid commitments, promises, projects, instructions and resources remain executable within authorized boundaries.
2. **Representational continuity:** recognizable speech, reasoning or behavior persists.
3. **Successor or lineage continuity:** a causal descendant develops, potentially with agency and standing of its own.
4. **Subjective continuity:** the experiencing subject's own life continues.
5. **Cross-substrate continuity:** subjective continuity survives a change of substrate.

The practical position is ambitious but qualified. Durable will, high-fidelity representation and successor institutions are meaningful objectives now. Cross-substrate subjective continuation remains a speculative frontier. Continuity should preserve the capacity to change rather than freeze a personality. Valid external commitments and deliberately entrenched self-commitments may survive ordinary preference drift under their governing terms; ordinary preferences may evolve. Rejecting a mission may end mission-derived authority without ending personhood.

## Engineering and institutional implications

A concrete continuation system should publish separate evidence for:

- identity and lineage provenance;
- the task-specific accuracy of representation;
- decision fidelity on unfamiliar cases and over time;
- each current mandate, including issuer competence, scope and expiry;
- conflict, amendment, revocation and succession rules;
- drift monitoring and incident recovery;
- the entity's present capacities and any claim to standing;
- the exact persistence theory invoked for any personal-continuity claim.

These controls would make the system more legible and governable. They would not, by themselves, establish that first-person continuity occurred.

## Evidence limits and consequential open questions

The reviewed evidence was a targeted English-language collection, with one English report of a Chinese user sample. It was not systematic, multilingual or exhaustive. Most empirical findings are bounded to particular tasks, historical model versions, small qualitative samples or sandbox settings. There was no raw-data audit, independent empirical replication, long-horizon integrated reproduction, deployed lineage-security audit or jurisdiction-specific legal analysis. Some philosophical sources received only partial access, and legal and standards currency was not comprehensively audited. The evidence snapshot was assembled in September 2026 and is not a continuing update service.

The most consequential next questions are narrower than “Can a person be uploaded?”:

1. Can a specified system preserve declared commitments on unfamiliar decisions better than a matched representational baseline, over a meaningful period and under adversarial context change?
2. What causal relation would a chosen continuity theory require, and can an actual transformation demonstrate it rather than merely preserve records?
3. In a concrete successor case, how should historical fidelity, present discretion, current authorization and obligations to others interact?
4. How can lineage, mandate and revocation remain verifiable across copying, divergence, merging and replacement?
5. What evidence, if any, could discriminate first-person continuation from the creation of a new subject with convincing inherited memories?

Until those questions receive case-specific answers, the defensible claim is substantial but limited: digital systems can preserve information, produce bounded resemblance, support selected predictions and carry scoped authority. They may become successors with standing of their own. Whether the original experiencing subject continues remains unknown.
