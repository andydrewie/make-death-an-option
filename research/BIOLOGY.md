# Biological continuity: demonstrated capabilities and the unclosed gap

## The question

What does current evidence show about slowing deterioration, restoring lost function, repairing or replacing biological components, and maintaining a living organism over time? Where do demonstrated finite results stop supporting claims about repeatable whole-organism maintenance or indefinite lifespan?

Here, **biological continuity** means continued organismal function through biological maintenance. It does not answer whether autobiographical identity, subjective continuity, numerical identity, or personhood persists. Those are separate questions.

The reviewed evidence supports several real capabilities:

- particular human interventions improve named functional, disease, or survival outcomes in selected populations over finite follow-up;
- specified animal protocols extend survival or improve function under specified conditions;
- some lost functions and biological components can be restored, regenerated, preserved, or replaced;
- some biomarkers predict outcomes, can be measured reliably, and respond to intervention;
- repeated dosing and some repeat replacement procedures are possible over bounded periods.

The evidence does not yet establish a general intervention that slows systemic human aging, a validated biomarker that converts treatment-induced change into added life, comprehensive repair of all essential systems, tolerable maintenance across arbitrary cycles, or indefinite lifespan. The gap is the conjunction: all essential structures must remain maintainable, the repair machinery must itself remain functional, components must continue to integrate, consequential failures must be detected and controlled, and cumulative harms must remain tolerable.

## Human evidence: finite outcomes in specified populations

Human clinical evidence is strongest when the claim remains tied to the studied population, comparator, endpoint, and follow-up.

The LIFE randomized trial included 1,635 sedentary adults aged 70–89 who had functional limitations but could walk 400 metres at baseline. Over a mean 2.6 years, major mobility disability occurred in 30.1% of the physical-activity group and 35.5% of the comparison group, hazard ratio 0.82, 95% CI [0.69, 0.98]. This demonstrates prevention or delay of a named disability outcome. It does not measure total lifespan or summarize health across every organ system. [LIFE trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4266388/)

REHAB-HF randomized 349 older adults hospitalized with heart failure. At three months, the between-group difference on the Short Physical Performance Battery was 1.5 points, 95% CI [0.9, 2.0]. Because participants began with measured impairment, this is evidence of functional recovery beyond control recovery over a finite interval, rather than only slower decline. It is still rehabilitation in a defined clinical population, not a demonstration that biological age was generally reversed. [REHAB-HF](https://cci-cic.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/REHAB-HF.pdf)

SPRINT randomized 9,361 adults at elevated cardiovascular risk who did not have diabetes or prior stroke. During a median 3.33 years of intervention follow-up, intensive blood-pressure treatment was associated with lower all-cause mortality, hazard ratio 0.75, 95% CI [0.61, 0.92]. The result is a finite survival benefit in a selected risk population. It does not by itself identify a general aging-rate intervention. The reviewed evidence did not include a complete SPRINT safety extraction. [SPRINT mortality report](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1901281)

The Da Qing study followed adults with impaired glucose tolerance after a six-year lifestyle intervention. At 30 years, the reported mortality hazard ratio was 0.74, 95% CI [0.61, 0.89], and the reported survival-area estimate corresponded to a life-expectancy gain of 1.44 years, 95% CI [0.20, 2.68]. The life-years estimate came from the reported survival analysis, not from converting a hazard ratio directly into years. Cluster design, ongoing care, adherence, and the selected population limit generalization. [Da Qing follow-up](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/106845/cdc_106845_DS1.pdf)

SELECT studied people with obesity and established cardiovascular disease. Semaglutide reduced the prespecified cardiovascular composite, hazard ratio 0.80, 95% CI [0.72, 0.90]. Later mortality estimates were not confirmatory after the statistical hierarchy failed at cardiovascular death. A meaningful cardiovascular outcome in a selected population is not equivalent to a general human-lifespan or global-healthspan effect. The reviewed safety extraction was incomplete. [SELECT](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10184940/1/nejmoa2307563.pdf)

These positive results are balanced by important nulls and harms. ASPREE found no improvement in disability-free survival with aspirin, hazard ratio 1.01, 95% CI [0.92, 1.11], while major bleeding increased, hazard ratio 1.38, 95% CI [1.18, 1.62]. [ASPREE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6426126/) DPPOS detected no mortality benefit over a median 21 years for metformin, hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI [0.79, 1.25], or lifestyle intervention, hazard ratio 1.02, 95% CI [0.81, 1.28]. Treatment convergence and low event rates constrain those estimates, but they still prevent automatic promotion of disease prevention into survival benefit. [DPPOS](https://gwern.net/doc/longevity/metformin/2021-lee-2.pdf)

Early geroscience interventions remain mixed. A later RTB101 trial failed its primary clinical endpoint despite a biological interferon-response signal, illustrating that a plausible mechanism or responsive marker does not guarantee clinical benefit. [RTB101](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568(21)00062-3) In a rapamycin-plus-exercise study, the combination did not add benefit on the primary chair-stand endpoint and raised the possibility of attenuated adaptation, though it did not prove universal harm. [Rapamycin and exercise](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jcsm.70274) In MASTERS, metformin blunted measured hypertrophic adaptation to resistance training; the reviewed evidence did not establish consequent effects on strength or disability. [MASTERS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6826125/)

**Human conclusion:** finite clinical, functional, and selected-population survival benefits are demonstrated. General human lifespan extension, global healthspan improvement, and general systemic aging-rate modification are not established by these outcomes.

## Animal evidence: stronger lifespan demonstrations, unresolved translation

Animal studies can directly measure lifespan under controlled protocols, but their results cannot be treated as human outcomes.

In a three-site genetically heterogeneous mouse study, rapamycin at 42 ppm beginning at nine months increased median survival from 807 to 992 days in males and from 896 to 1,132 days in females. Corresponding 90th-percentile survival measures also increased. These are whole-age medians and upper-tail quantiles, not maximum possible lifespan. Multiple sites strengthen robustness within the cohort, but shared controls, laboratories, and repeated reports do not independently replicate every schedule or mechanism. [Rapamycin in UM-HET3 mice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4032600/)

Other animal protocols show finite positive effects under more qualified conditions. Dietary restriction in diversity-outbred female mice produced a substantial survival signal alongside lean-mass and immune tradeoffs. [Di Francesco et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08026-3) Late-life anti-IL11 treatment produced mouse survival and functional benefits, though independent therapeutic lifespan replication was not established. [Widjaja et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07701-9) Dasatinib plus quercetin begun at 24–27 months was reported to increase median **remaining** life by 36%; this is not a 36% increase in total lifespan. [Xu et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-018-0092-9)

Contrary findings constrain generalization. Rhesus-monkey caloric-restriction studies at the University of Wisconsin and the National Institute on Aging were discordant: a survival benefit in one program coexisted with nonsignificance in the other. Feeding, age of onset, husbandry, and care are possible explanations, not a randomized resolution of the disagreement. [Rhesus-monkey study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5247583/) Some rapamycin-treated mice lived longer while many age-sensitive traits did not improve and toxicity remained. Cyclic and continuous fisetin schedules were null in one set of protocols, and 2026 Intervention Testing Program results were null for several compounds under their tested doses and schedules. Changed dose, onset, and achieved exposure prevent those nulls from becoming universal refutations. [ITP 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10828146/) [ITP 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13356140/)

A preliminary AAV-OSK result in exceptionally old male mice reported a 109% increase in remaining survival, not a doubling of whole lifespan. Control, denominator, precision, and replication questions remained unresolved in the reviewed evidence. [AAV-OSK preliminary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10909732/) Combination studies without concurrent factorial constituent arms do not establish synergy or additivity.

Animal effects can depend on sex, dose, achieved exposure, strain, diet, pathogens, husbandry, censoring, and the dominant lethal disease. Human translation requires relevant exposure, tolerability, meaningful clinical endpoints, and adequately powered human trials. Conserved mechanisms alone do not close that bridge.

**Animal conclusion:** specified protocols demonstrate finite survival effects in animals. Their generality varies, and human translation remains insufficient.

## Slowing decline is different from restoring function

Slowing deterioration means changing the rate or probability of future loss. Restoration requires an impaired preintervention state, regained relevant function relative to a credible comparator, and a stated duration. Restoration does not necessarily mean returning a person or animal to a measured youthful state.

Human rehabilitation, described above, meets a functional-restoration comparison over three months. Component-level biological interventions provide additional examples:

- Genetically corrected epidermal stem cells regenerated a functional epidermis after catastrophic skin loss in one child, with tissue integrity reported through 21 months. This establishes a powerful local regenerative capability in a pediatric monogenic-disease setting, not natural-aging reversal. [Epidermal regeneration](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature24487)
- Limbal stem-cell treatment restored renewing corneal epithelium in selected damaged eyes, with variable follow-up. This is local tissue renewal, with access-limited methods in the reviewed record. [Limbal-cell treatment](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0905955)
- An early uncontrolled stem-cell-derived islet study reported one-year endocrine outcomes in 12 full-dose recipients, including clinical glucose, hypoglycaemia, insulin, and C-peptide outcomes. Fourteen participants contributed to reported safety, including two half-dose and 12 full-dose participants. The 12-person efficacy group must not be described as all 14 safety participants. Later deaths at roughly 19.5 and 30 months do not explain the 14/12 analysis split, demonstrate survival benefit, or prove that the cell product caused the deaths. Enrollment flow and a later source-revision difference remained unresolved. [Stem-cell-derived islets](https://www.surgery.pitt.edu/sites/default/files/Reichman%20VX-880%20NEJM%202025.pdf)
- FDA-approved donor-islet treatment permits one to three infusions, demonstrating bounded repeat component administration. Its serious adverse-reaction burden and immunosuppressive treatment context remain part of the intervention. [Lantidra label](https://www.fda.gov/media/169920/download)
- Kidney retransplantation after graft failure is direct evidence that component replacement can sometimes be repeated. The reported survival association arose in selected waitlisted people and remained observational; the reviewed evidence did not support unrestricted causal effect size, general eligibility, or arbitrary repeat cycles. [Kidney retransplantation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajt.2023.01.008)
- Engineered heart muscle produced vascularized tissue augmentation over six months in macaques and anatomical remuscularization in an early human case. A macaque ejection-fraction result was responder-selected rather than an all-treated average. One perioperative death, graft rejection after immune withdrawal, and ectopic tissue prevent promotion to controlled clinical benefit or comprehensive cardiac repair. [Engineered heart muscle](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08463-0)
- Nanowarmed rat kidneys stored for as long as 100 days sustained nephrectomized recipients for 30 days. This is life-supporting component preservation and replacement, not whole-body revival or established long-term safety. A nonsignificant comparison at 30 days is not proof of equivalence. [Kidney preservation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-38824-8)

Local integration or anatomical persistence is not automatically net clinical benefit. An older fetal-graft randomized trial in Parkinson's disease found no overall motor benefit despite graft survival or imaging evidence. [Fetal-graft trial](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.10720) DREAM-HF likewise reported a null primary clinical endpoint despite a regenerative rationale. [DREAM-HF](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2022.11.061) Modern Parkinson's graft cohorts remain early, uncontrolled, or mixed, and one fatal disseminated infection during immunosuppression keeps supportive-treatment harm inside the assessment. [STEM-PD](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04525-0)

Anatomical persistence, vascularization, secretion, imaging activity, restored local function, and net patient benefit are separate integration steps. Demonstrating one does not establish the rest.

**Component conclusion:** repair, regeneration, preservation, and replacement are demonstrated at selected cell, tissue, and organ scales. Simultaneous integration across heterogeneous essential systems, maintenance of host coordination, and comprehensive organism-wide repair remain unproved.

## Biomarker changes do not establish clinical benefit

Biomarker performance has at least four distinct levels: analytical reliability, prediction of future outcomes, response to an intervention, and validated prediction of the intervention's net clinical effect. Success at an earlier level does not automatically establish the next.

DunedinPACE predicted mortality and cardiovascular outcomes in cohorts separate from its training cohort. Reported mortality hazard ratios per standard deviation were 1.65, 95% CI [1.51, 1.79], in the Framingham Heart Study and 1.26, 95% CI [1.14, 1.40], in the Normative Aging Study. This supports prognosis, not treatment surrogacy. [DunedinPACE validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8853656/) Later work improved technical reliability, another useful but distinct achievement. [Reliability study](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1kk8q3wv/qt1kk8q3wv.pdf)

In a randomized CALERIE ancillary analysis, DunedinPACE responded over 12 and 24 months while other clocks were null. This supports intervention responsiveness for one measure in one setting. It does not convert clock change into added years of life. [CALERIE clock analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-022-00357-y.pdf)

DO-HEALTH sharpens the distinction. An exploratory ancillary analysis in 777 selected Swiss participants reported nominal patterns of marker response without a single prespecified primary clock and without established multiplicity-controlled significance. [DO-HEALTH marker analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-024-00793-y) In the 2,157-person parent trial, no benefit was detected across six primary clinical outcomes under its stated *p* < .01 and 99% confidence-interval threshold. [DO-HEALTH clinical outcomes](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2772758) The samples, estimands, sensitivity, and tests differ, so this contrast neither proves individual-level surrogate failure nor validates surrogacy.

Other dissociations matter. RTB101 produced a biological response despite later failure on a clinical primary endpoint. A Parkinson's graft can persist anatomically without improving the overall clinical endpoint. Clock values can also vary with cell composition, proliferation, and acute stress. These observations challenge automatic promotion from marker to outcome while leaving open the possibility that a particular biomarker could eventually be validated for a particular intervention and population.

The missing bridge is demanding: treatment-induced biomarker change must predict net, clinically meaningful effects in the relevant population and intervention setting under aligned temporal and causal assumptions, with adequate independent validation. Mediation through one measured pathway may miss off-pathway harm. No reviewed result warrants converting clock months or pace percentages directly into life extension.

## Harm, interactions, and the burden of maintenance

Maintenance must be assessed as a whole intervention, including surgery, immunosuppression, repeated procedures, drug interactions, monitoring, and failures. Product-specific attribution does not remove these burdens from the person's experience.

The reviewed human evidence includes major bleeding with aspirin, possible attenuation of exercise adaptation with rapamycin, blunted hypertrophic adaptation with metformin, serious adverse reactions with donor-islet treatment, infection during immunosuppression, perioperative death, and graft rejection after immune withdrawal. Small studies that detect no tumors cannot establish lifetime oncogenic safety. Animal evidence adds dose-, schedule-, and expression-dependent toxicity, including harms in reprogramming and cell-ablation paradigms, although some relevant source access was partial and does not support a universal quantitative risk estimate.

Repeated exposure is not the same as repeatable maintenance. Completing a finite course, sustaining a graft for a period, repeating a dose, replacing an organ a second time, and demonstrating an independently replicated cycle are different achievements. A procedure that works once may fail after host biology changes. Combinations cannot be assumed additive, and repeated interventions may interact with exercise, immunity, healing, or one another.

**Safety conclusion:** some bounded interventions have a favorable net benefit under studied conditions. Open-ended tolerability, compatible combinations, and cumulative safety across successive heterogeneous repairs remain insufficiently established.

## From finite repair to whole-organism maintenance

Finite successes make maintenance engineering a serious question. They do not yet demonstrate its complete architecture. An open-ended biological-maintenance claim would require evidence for all of the following:

1. **Repair capacity exceeds consequential damage.** Selected repairs and functional gains are demonstrated, but there is no comprehensive accounting of damage generation versus repair capacity across systems and ages.
2. **The repair machinery remains maintainable.** Renewing epidermal and corneal grafts show local finite renewal. Joint preservation of stem-cell reserves, immune surveillance, and repair infrastructure over an open horizon is unestablished.
3. **Every essential system remains repairable, replaceable, or recoverable in time.** Tissue, islet, and kidney examples establish capability at selected scales. Comprehensive coverage of essential structures was not assessed.
4. **Components remain integrated and organismal coordination remains viable.** Endocrine function, renal life support, and local myocardial integration are supported within scope. Simultaneous heterogeneous systemic integration is not.
5. **Successive maintenance retains efficacy.** Repeat islet administration, kidney retransplantation, and finite repeated dosing show bounded cycle capability. Efficacy across arbitrary cycles and altered host biology is unestablished.
6. **Cumulative tradeoffs remain tolerable.** Protocol-specific net benefits exist, but immune, procedural, oncogenic, metabolic, and interaction risks remain.
7. **Consequential failures can be detected and controlled.** Prognostic biomarkers and measured clinical endpoints help, but a predictive clock does not ensure causal, timely detection and control of every dangerous failure.
8. **Emergent failures remain recoverable while net organismal function persists.** Individual recovery examples do not establish future failure classes, response time, or universal repairability.

No finite observation can empirically demonstrate an infinite horizon. That logical limit does not make engineering inference illegitimate, and it does not prove that indefinite maintenance is impossible. It means that feasibility depends on justified assumptions about repair coverage, maintainable repair machinery, coordination, control, and cumulative risk. The reviewed evidence does not establish their conjunction.

Indefinite maintenance capacity would also differ from guaranteed survival. Even a maintainable organism could remain vulnerable to external hazards. Biological maintenance, if achieved, would still not answer whether the same first-person subject continues.

## Disease treatment and systemic aging

The evidence does not support a purely definitional separation in which treating blood pressure, glucose regulation, cardiovascular risk, or another disease can never affect aging biology. Disease pathways may overlap processes associated with aging. At the same time, systemic exposure, an aging-target label, multisystem biomarkers, or an all-cause-mortality endpoint do not uniquely demonstrate modification of a general aging rate.

A stronger systemic-aging attribution would need convergent meaningful outcomes across relevant systems, a warranted shared mechanism, appropriate timing and duration, serious treatment of competing explanations, replication, and population transport. These are evidentiary considerations rather than one universally settled test. The present evidence leaves broader systemic effects plausible but does not uniquely establish them.

## Evidence limits and the next questions

This assessment used bounded public English-language evidence available through 13 September 2026. It was not an exhaustive systematic review, raw-data reanalysis, complete regulatory survey, or complete correction and retraction audit. Some publisher pages, full texts, supplements, denominator details, safety records, and updated source versions were inaccessible or unresolved. Current findings within the cutoff were considered, but later repository changes were not fully authenticated to the historical cutoff.

Study families reuse participants, cohorts, laboratories, and controls. LIFE reports share participants; the DO-HEALTH marker subset sits inside its parent trial; CALERIE analyses reuse trial participants; clock studies reuse several cohorts; Intervention Testing Program reports can share controls. Publication count is therefore not a replication count.

The most useful empirical work would target the missing bridges rather than repeat broad claims. Priorities include matched biomarker-and-clinical-outcome validation within the same intervention and population; causal studies of repeated replacement with complete denominators and treatment burdens; long follow-up that distinguishes local persistence from net function; explicit assessment of interactions among maintenance interventions; and system-level models that state which essential functions are covered, how the repair machinery is maintained, how failures are detected, and what would falsify the architecture.

The present record justifies ambition about biological maintenance because meaningful human outcomes, animal survival effects, restoration, regeneration, and replacement are real. It also requires precision: these are finite, heterogeneous capabilities. Their integration into repeatable whole-organism maintenance, much less an indefinite lifespan, remains an open engineering and scientific problem.
