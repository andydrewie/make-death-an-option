# Follow one claim to its limits

Can reviewing a camera record make a later account more accurate? This path follows one reported positive result, its limits, contrary evidence and a decision that remains open. It assembles navigation from the public September 2026 assessment. It is **not a fresh source audit, replication or DPoC pilot**. The [evidence dates and access limits](RESEARCH.md#evidence-access-and-currency) still apply.

## The claim and its source

The [memory assessment, “Some procedures improve narrow, checkable outcomes”](research/MEMORY.md#some-procedures-improve-narrow-checkable-outcomes) reports that a randomized body-camera review procedure improved final statement accuracy after a simulated theft in virtual reality. Statements were blind-coded; the reported improvement involved more correct and fewer incorrect details.

The source is the [body-camera study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0243226), tracked as **memory-body-camera-vr** in the [memory and biology source index](research/source-index-memory-biology.json). Its recorded scientific check date is 13 September 2026. The [source navigation table](SOURCES.md#memory) preserves the access qualifications.

## What that result can carry

The assessment supports a **procedure-specific reporting effect**. Its factual scope remains attached to these limits:

- The study included **97 participants aged 17–42**, with no adult-only subgroup result available.
- The event was a **VR theft**, not ordinary lived autobiography.
- The **exact accuracy denominator is unknown** in the assessment. Only **10 statements were second-coded**; blind coding does not remove that reliability limit.
- Footage review can change reporting through relearning or selection. Some correct details were removed. A smaller share of off-camera details does not establish an absolute loss of those details.

These observations do not establish a general adult autobiographical accuracy benefit, recovery of the original experience or personal continuity. “Unknown” stays unknown; a source link cannot fill in the missing denominator.

## The strongest complication

The [memory assessment, “Strongest contrary and complicating evidence”](research/MEMORY.md#strongest-contrary-and-complicating-evidence), retains **memory-museum-reactivation** beside the favorable result. In that [museum study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4339638/), reactivation paired with related misleading information increased both true endorsements and false alarms in younger and older groups. The primary table was checked, but the strict minimum participant age was unverified and no signal-detection or mechanism reanalysis was performed.

This is a **different procedure**, not a direct refutation of the body-camera result or a measurement of everyday risk from authentic, unsuggested photo review. It does defeat the easy inference that more remembered material necessarily means fewer errors. The [first objection](OBJECTIONS.md) challenges the further leap from vivid reconstruction to accuracy and original experience.

## What could change the assessment

[Q-MEM-01 · Accuracy after access](QUESTIONS.md#q-mem-01--accuracy-after-access) asks when authentic-record review improves independently verified adult autobiographical accuracy. **CC-01 · Check the strongest camera-accuracy inference**, in [Three concrete contribution candidates](QUESTIONS.md#three-concrete-contribution-candidates), identifies the immediate missing methods: exact denominator, coding reliability, adult-only availability and what review permits learning.

A later, separately scoped check could support any of these decisions:

| Decision | What it would mean |
|---|---|
| Retain | Keep the narrow reporting claim if the checked methods support it. |
| Narrow | Restrict the wording further to the population, procedure and outcome actually established. |
| Withhold | Leave an accuracy claim unestablished when necessary scoring information cannot be recovered. |
| Correct | Amend an assessment detail if a verified source discrepancy changes it, preserving the prior version. |
