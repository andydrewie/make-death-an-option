# Questions worth resolving

These questions arise from the bounded assessments and the article's proposed institutions. They are research directions, not findings or permission to launch work. IDs remain stable when wording is clarified. Current outside intake is described in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Q-MEM-01 · Accuracy after access

**State:** unresolved empirical question. **Beneficiary:** readers and designers distinguishing recall support from faithful reconstruction.

**Question:** under what adult autobiographical-memory procedures does review of an authentic external record improve independently verified accuracy, rather than only recall quantity or reporting based on the record?

The [existing assessment](research/MEMORY.md) supports cueing and selected checkable outcomes, but procedures, comparators and scoring denominators differ. Some positive evidence concerns staged or mixed-age samples. Confidence, vividness, event occurrence, details and original experience remain separate outcomes.

**Missing discriminator:** an explicit independent comparator, correct/error/omission denominator and a design separating retrieval from learning what the record shows. Cue-absent delayed performance would answer a stronger question than final reporting with access to the record.

**Useful output:** a small evidence table identifying the strongest positive result, its exact target and comparator, the strongest contrary result and the narrowest claim justified by both. A finding that the claimed discriminator is absent is useful.

**Consequence:** changes what a reconstruction service may claim and which fidelity experiment should be attempted next. It does not settle subjective continuity.

## Q-BIO-01 · The next maintenance cycle

**State:** unresolved empirical and engineering question. **Beneficiary:** researchers assessing repeated maintenance rather than isolated repair.

**Question:** for one specified replacement or restoration procedure, what evidence establishes efficacy and net harm across a subsequent cycle after the host has changed?

The [biology assessment](research/BIOLOGY.md) documents finite component successes and some repeat administration or replacement. These do not establish arbitrary repeatability, compatible combinations or whole-organism maintenance.

**Missing discriminator:** complete cycle denominators, time-dependent selection, function and adverse outcomes, supporting-treatment burdens and relevant untreated or alternative comparators.

**Useful output:** one procedure-specific causal diagram and evidence dossier that distinguishes measured repeat-cycle benefit from selection and survivorship. No complete longevity theory is required.

**Consequence:** identifies a concrete maintenance bottleneck and prevents an isolated intervention from standing in for an integrated architecture.

## Q-ID-01 · Fidelity across divergence

**State:** unresolved technical question with philosophical limits. **Beneficiary:** people delegating to a representation or successor and counterparties relying on it.

**Question:** can a specified system preserve declared commitments on unfamiliar decisions over time better than a matched persona baseline, while reliably distinguishing an accurate prediction from current permission?

The [identity assessment](research/IDENTITY.md) supports bounded prediction and documents fidelity and drift limits. It does not validate long-horizon personal-value preservation, generic revocation propagation or subjective survival.

**Missing discriminator:** a frozen commitment target, unseen decisions, a matched baseline, changes in authority and a way to separate legitimate amendment from fidelity failure.

**Useful output:** a scoped evaluation design, then an explicitly authorized test. Behavioral fidelity would remain evidence about a task, not a measurement of a person reconstructed.

**Consequence:** constrains delegation claims and may reveal that a representation requires narrower authority or more review.

The conceptual analysis [Continuity Through Self-Improvement](SELF-IMPROVEMENT.md) develops the prior question of what a fidelity target should preserve when knowledge and methods change. Its fictional case supplies an argument, not an executed evaluation.

## Q-ID-02 · What could distinguish continuation from succession?

**State:** unresolved philosophical and evidentiary question. **Beneficiary:** people assessing claims about persistence of an experiencing subject across transformation.

**Question:** for one specified transformation, what would distinguish continuation of the original subject from a successor with matching records, behavior and reported memories?

The [identity assessment](research/IDENTITY.md) distinguishes representation and lineage from first-person survival. The [continuity thresholds](ATLAS.md#six-continuity-thresholds) retain the stronger ambition without treating resemblance as its demonstration. A theory may require a relation that available observations cannot establish; that limitation must remain visible.

**Missing discriminator:** specify the competing persistence criteria, the observation each predicts, and whether the difference is accessible to an observer, only to a subject, or not operationally specified. Gradual replacement, interruption and restart, copying, and branching are distinct candidate comparisons; select one before designing a study. A verbal claim of continuity cannot serve as its own independent validation.

**Useful output:** a bounded comparison separating a theory's required relation, available evidence, rival interpretations and residual underdetermination. “No discriminating observation identified” is a legitimate result. The comparison should also identify what the available evidence can establish about memory, function or lineage without promoting those results into proof of survival.

**Consequence:** changes which continuity claims can be responsibly offered and which further investigation could matter. Consent, legal status, administrative designation and a successor's possible standing remain separate from a measurement of the original subject's persistence.

## Q-CA-01 · Does the wider frame improve a decision?

**State:** unresolved comparative hypothesis. **Beneficiary:** institutions choosing among consequential courses of action.

**Question:** in one named decision, does Civilizational Alignment identify a decision-relevant omission beyond a strong capabilities, broad-alignment or governance analysis?

The [alignment assessment](research/ALIGNMENT.md) preserves the integrative proposal and its strongest redundancy objection. Naming more considerations is not sufficient evidence of improved judgment.

**Missing discriminator:** the same case and source set; strong rival analyses; declared criteria for materially changed options, overlooked affected parties, actionable safeguards, delay and review burden. Disagreement about values must remain distinguishable from factual omission.

**Useful output:** a matched case comparison with a reasoned decision delta, including a legitimate finding of no additional value.

**Consequence:** informs whether the framework deserves routine use, narrower use or conceptual retention without operational superiority claims.

## Q-UA-01 · When a civilization can create new beings

**State:** future conceptual research candidate; not an active mission. **Beneficiary:** a possible society in which participants can create additional beings with morally relevant interests.

**Question:** if some participants can create additional potentially conscious beings, how should their standing, representation and material claims be considered without making reproductive capacity a route to domination or treating dependent beings as disposable?

The [alignment assessment](research/ALIGNMENT.md) preserves expansion, dignity, agency and plurality as commitments to examine. Their interaction under artificial reproduction is a further question, not a demonstrated feature of current systems. The [frontier framing](FRONTIERS.md#when-a-civilization-can-create-new-beings) states the conditional scenario.

**Missing discriminator:** distinguish moral standing from institutional membership, voting power, resource entitlement and creator responsibility. Compare rules under the same stipulated constraints, including effects on already existing dependants and on newly created claimants. Do not assume that each software instance is conscious or that instance count measures moral weight.

**Useful output:** one case with competing institutional responses, explicit premises about standing and scarcity, and a reasoned account of who bears each response's cost. Empirical claims about consciousness or replication would require separately scoped evidence.

**Consequence:** could constrain proposed expansion or representation rules before an institution relies on them. It does not issue reproductive, coercive or resource-allocation authority.

## Q-INST-01 · Refusal when exit is not enough

**State:** unresolved institutional question. **Beneficiary:** continuations, dependants and counterparties subject to long-lived commitments.

**Question:** how should a particular institution handle a competent later-self refusal when another person's valid claim depends on continued service and no adequate alternative is immediately available?

The [atlas](ATLAS.md) and [cases](CASES.md) distinguish personhood, ordinary preference, entrenched commitment, office, mission assets and relationships. They do not supply a universal hardship rule or jurisdiction-specific legal answer.

**Missing discriminator:** exact undertaking, affected parties, alternatives, time sensitivity, authority, feasible relief and the consequences of both forced continuation and abrupt withdrawal.

**Useful output:** a bounded comparison of defensible resolutions and their unresolved costs. A real service would additionally require applicable professional review.

**Consequence:** may change the service or commitment offered in the first place. A polished interface cannot remove this conflict.

## Q-FRONT-01 · One bottleneck that changes the frontier

**State:** direction awaiting a specific selection. **Beneficiary:** a defined continuity or abundance research effort.

**Question:** which one constrained capability, in a named workload, most limits a useful next step and admits a discriminating investigation?

The [frontier map](FRONTIERS.md) preserves grounded and breakthrough ambitions across sensing, memory, compute, materials, energy and institutions. It makes no unresearched claim about the current best technology.

**Missing discriminator:** a workload, current comparator, integration cost, beneficiary and result that could redirect effort. A technology roundup would not resolve it.

**Useful output:** one bottleneck dossier with a testable alternative and reasons to investigate, narrow or stop.

**Consequence:** directs compute toward a consequential limit while retaining uncertain, potentially transformative paths.

A useful contribution may contradict the article's empirical expectations. It should make an assessment or next decision better, not merely add citations or supporting language. New experiments, participant contact, private data collection and external acts require their own scope and permission.
