01A photograph, with one blank left intactA MEMORY · FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION

THE READER BRINGS

I’m sorting an old family picnic photo. The envelope says June 2008. In the photo, my aunt is pouring lemonade, my cousin is holding a red ball, and the picnic table is leaning. I remember everyone joking about that table, but I don’t remember who took the picture. Please use only this description. I want a caption that preserves what I know without filling in the blank.

FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION. A fictional reader supplies a description and a recollection. The agent has not inspected the photograph. This is written sample copy, not a real user output or a model test.

THE REFLECTION

A photograph offers one view of a picnic. Being there can also involve a sense of where the body is and how it moves; the image alone cannot tell us how that picnic felt to you. If it suits you, pause with one detail available in the photograph or your description.

The envelope still carries a date. The person behind the camera has no name in your account. Between those two things, there is a leaning table, your aunt pouring lemonade, and your cousin with a red ball. I’m working from your description, without having inspected the image. The table is a small detail to have kept, but in your recollection it opens onto everyone joking about it. Something outside the still picture begins to return.

That is the movement in §1, We Already Resist Disappearance: a photograph becomes a way back into a larger world. Here, your caption could keep that opening available. §13, What Happens When Memory Lies? adds a useful form of care: leave room for a later reader to know which detail came from the record and which came from remembering.

Family picnic. The envelope is marked “June 2008.” Aunt pouring lemonade; my cousin with a red ball; the table leaning. I remember us joking about the table. I don’t remember who took the photograph.

The blank has a place in that caption. So does the part you can still recall. If you choose to keep it beside the photograph, a later version of you could return to both, perhaps adding something, perhaps leaving the question as it is. You do not need to make the account complete to preserve this much of it. This caption is ready to copy; it has not been saved by an action here.

02An old plan, and room to changeAN UNFINISHED PROJECT · FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION

THE READER BRINGS

Today’s date is 2026-09-22. Please use just this project. In 2022 I planned a weekly newsletter about local walking routes. I wrote six drafts and never launched. The note at the top says, “Help people notice their neighborhood.” I still want that, but I no longer want a weekly deadline or a public audience. I never promised anyone I would publish. Help me decide what is worth carrying forward.

FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION. A fictional reader selects one old project and supplies all relevant context. The date below is supplied within the fictional scenario. This is written sample copy, not a real user output or a model test.

THE REFLECTION

If useful, pause on the opening sentence of the old plan. The words have stayed on the page while you have kept living; what you notice in them now may differ from what first made you write them.

“Help people notice their neighborhood.” That part still reaches you. The weekly deadline and the public audience no longer do. It seems possible that the project’s purpose has lasted longer than the form you first gave it. The six drafts you describe belong to that earlier attempt; they need not decide what the interest becomes next.

§10, Preserve Continuity, Not Sameness gives this change room to breathe. Keeping the original note would let a later you see why the newsletter began. Adding today’s understanding would let them see why its shape changed. You say you made no promise to publish, so there is no stated promise asking to be renegotiated here. The old plan can remain part of your history while you reconsider its future.

In §14, The Continuity Institution, a project carries more than files. It carries reasons, connections and paths that might remain useful. A walking newsletter might become a private collection of routes, or simply leave you with a question you take outside: what is here that I have stopped noticing? Those are possible forms, not conclusions about what you ought to want.

One small way to find out would be to make a private walking note from whatever material you still have, then use it on an outing you choose. You could see whether the interest comes alive in that form. You could also leave the project where it is. What seems worth carrying forward is the aim you still recognize, with enough of its history to understand how you arrived here. This reflection has not changed or saved your project files.

03One small item is enough to beginA QUESTION · FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION

THE READER BRINGS

The invitation is pasted without a chosen item or permission to retrieve other context.

THE AGENT ASKS ONCE

Human perception reaches beyond sight and sound: a sense of body position and balance, and signals from within the body, are also part of how a moment reaches us. If useful, pause with one detail available to you now; there is no need to report it. I don’t have a personal item to work with yet. What is one small memory, unfinished project, or question you would like to bring?

THE READER CHOOSES

At dinner with friends last weekend, I kept photographing the food. Looking back, I wish I had followed the conversation more closely. I still like having the photos. Can I keep a record and be there more next time?

FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION. The agent has no prior personal context. It asks once, waits for a fictional reply, and only then writes the bounded reflection. This is written sample copy, not a real user output or a model test.

THE REFLECTION

The photographs let you look back at the dinner from where the camera was. You are looking from another point in your life now, noticing both your pleasure in having them and your wish to have followed more of the conversation.

There is something very recognizable in wanting to keep an evening while it is still happening. The record is for a later you, but making it takes place in the same minutes as the life you want to remember. In your account, both desires remain: you still like the photographs, and you want more room for the people at the table.

§3, The Fidelity Gap calls this competition the “Capture Tax.” The connection does not require deciding that taking the pictures was a mistake. It gives a name to the exchange you noticed. We cannot recover the parts of the conversation you missed from this account, or tell how much taking photographs contributed to that feeling. What we can carry forward is your wish to make the exchange differently next time.

One small experiment could be to take a photograph when you want one, then put the phone away for a stretch you choose. Later, you could ask whether you had a record you were glad to keep and more room to participate. There is no ideal number of photographs hidden in what you have told me. You can adjust the experiment or leave it aside; the next dinner can remain a dinner.

The question you brought is already precise enough to be useful: how to keep something of the evening while giving more of your attention to being there. This response is a draft you can copy, and I have not saved or exported it.
